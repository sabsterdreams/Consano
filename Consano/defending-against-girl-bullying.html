Build your shield against emotional abuse

Your shield is what protects your inner peace. It’s built from pieces of life lessons and wisdom. What’s great is that you can learn about these now to save you from going through more difficult experiences.

Standing up for yourself doesn’t have to look the same for every scenario.

Here are some shields you may want to add to your collection:

Shield 1: Calling Out Broken Boundaries

For when boundaries are broken, asking ‘What do you mean by that?’ or ‘What did you say?’ or ‘I don’t like being spoken to like that’ can help catch the behavior right in its tracks.

Shield 2: Seeking Tailored Support

This doesn’t have to be for telling the people off, but rather for getting more tailored advice and support for yourself from people you trust.

Shield 3: Expanding Your Circle

Knowing you have friends outside of school can make managing this so much easier as you feel more secure. Join a new club or start volunteering. Join free virtual or in person work experience programmes. These are great as they connect you safely to those you may not have ever met on your walk to school, but also give you skills that you can carry into your future and university applications. Plus, they serve as a good distraction from the drama.

Truth be told, sometimes people are just unlucky. It’s never your fault that you’re being treated poorly, as you don’t deserve to be.

What if I didn't do this when it happened?
Don’t beat yourself up if you have made mistakes in how you react to emotional abuse. You wasn’t expecting it, so if you’re not well equipped, you can’t blame yourself for doing what you could at the time. You’re still here, and that’s great!
What if it doesn't work?
Some people are so cunning and manipulative (or deeply insecure) that they are literally wired to seek chaos. This is why walking away and focusing on healing yourself can often be the best solution.
